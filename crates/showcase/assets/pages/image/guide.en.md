## When to use

Use an image for a picture the person chose or needs to see: a desktop wallpaper, the preview of a file, an avatar, a chart rendered elsewhere. It draws in every terminal with 256 colours or more, over SSH too, because it needs nothing but coloured cells; a terminal that speaks the kitty graphics protocol or sixel draws it itself, in real pixels.

## Step by step

1. Turn on the `image` feature of `quvyta-framework` in `Cargo.toml`: `features = ["image"]`.
2. Learn how the terminal draws pictures: implement `App::graphics`, which hears the `Graphics` in force before the first frame and again whenever it changes, and keep it in your state. Where `graphics.can_draw()` is false (16 colours, ASCII glyphs) there is nothing to decode; show your own quiet state or nothing, as a wallpaper does.
3. Decode the file in the background, since decoding a large photo takes a moment: `Command::perform(move || ImageData::decode_file(&path, (width, height * 2)), Msg::Decoded)`. Ask for the area's width in cells and twice its height, so only as many pixels as the screen can show are kept. When the graphics are `Graphics::Kitty` the terminal shows every pixel it is sent, so ask for more, about ten times the width and twenty times the height; the picture is sent at the size kept and never enlarged. Over a remote connection (`Env::remote()` in `view`, or `Env::remote_session()` before the runtime starts, when the decode size is chosen in `update`) every one of those pixels crosses the link once, and a grainy photo hardly compresses: there ask for about twice the half-block size, two pixels a cell across and four down, and let the terminal enlarge it smoothly. With `Graphics::Sixel` the picture is shrunk to the pixels of its cells on screen whatever you keep, so the kitty size is as sharp as it gets and anything larger only costs memory; over a remote link what crosses the wire is the size on screen, not the size you decoded. When `App::graphics` tells you of a change, decode again at the new size.
4. While it decodes, show a `Spinner`; when it fails, show an `EmptyState` with the error, which reads as a sentence: `error.to_string()`.
5. Draw it: `ui.add(Image::new(&data).fit(Fit::Cover)).fill()`.
6. For a picture built into the application, decode its bytes: `ImageData::decode_bytes(include_bytes!("logo.png"), max)`. For pixels you already have, use `ImageData::from_rgb(width, height, &bytes)`.
7. To let the person choose a picture, give the file picker the extensions the decoder reads: `FileBrowser::new(&folder, PickMode::Files).extensions(ImageData::EXTENSIONS.iter().copied())`.

## How it works

- **Two pixels a cell.** Every cell is drawn as `▀`: the upper pixel is the colour of the glyph, the lower one the background behind it. A cell is about twice as tall as it is wide, so the two halves are close to square and the picture keeps its shape.
- **Three fits.** `Contain` shows the whole picture as large as fits, leaving the ground beside it. `Cover` fills the whole area and cuts what spills over evenly from both sides; it is the one for wallpapers. `Center` shows the picture at its own size, one pixel per half cell, and cuts what does not fit.
- **Averaged, not picked.** Shrinking takes the average of every pixel a new pixel covers, so a photo keeps its tones instead of turning into noise.
- **Worked out once.** The cells are kept in the widget's memory and worked out again only when the area's size, the fit or the picture changes. A frame that draws the same picture again only copies them.
- **A ground to draw on.** Cells the picture does not reach keep what is under them, and anything painted after the picture lands on it, so windows and icons can sit on a wallpaper.
- **Real pixels where the terminal can.** When `Env::graphics` says `Kitty`, the widget paints no half blocks: its cells get the plain ground and the terminal draws the picture over them. The pixels cross the wire once and are kept under a number; a later frame only says where to show them, and a frame where nothing moved writes nothing at all. A picture no longer shown is freed from the terminal's memory.
- **Menus and windows stay on top.** The picture sits under text and over the cells' ground. Whatever is painted over it wins: the cells left showing are split into rectangles and the terminal shows the matching part of the picture in each, cut at the same pixel on both sides of every seam, so a menu along one side, an icon in the middle or a window over a wallpaper leave the rest in real pixels. Cells under a dialog's dimmed backdrop or a window's shadow are drawn with half blocks in the same frame, dimmed as the backdrop dims them, while the rest stays pixels. Moving a window over the picture only places its parts again; the pixels are not sent again. A picture cut into more than 64 pieces is drawn with half blocks for that frame.
- **Sixel, only whole.** When `Env::graphics` says `Sixel`, the terminal paints the picture into the cells, shrunk to the pixels those cells cover and reduced to 252 colours. Text written over a sixel wipes it, so it cannot be cut around a menu: while nothing at all is painted over the picture it is real pixels, and while a menu, a dialog or anything else lies over any of it the whole picture is drawn with half blocks. It is painted again when it comes back, when a cell under it changes, and after a program had the terminal; a frame where nothing changed writes nothing. The encoding is kept, so opening and closing a dialog over a wallpaper does not encode it again, but every return sends the picture again, a few hundred kilobytes for a full-screen photo.
- **Every terminal.** With 256 colours both halves of every cell take their nearest palette entry. With only the sixteen standard colours, or with ASCII glyphs, `Env::graphics` is `Graphics::None` and pixels are never drawn with characters: the picture's name, format and size are shown with a sentence saying that this terminal cannot show pictures. `Graphics::can_draw` is the widget's own test, so an application that would rather show nothing asks it first and gets the same answer.

## Common mistakes

- **Decoding in `view`.** A large photo takes a noticeable time to decode; do it once, in `Command::perform`, and keep the `ImageData` in your state.
- **Asking for the full picture.** A 4000 by 3000 photo kept whole is tens of megabytes. Pass the size the screen can show as `max`; only that is kept.
- **Copying the check.** Testing colour depth and glyph mode yourself misses `QUVYTA_GRAPHICS`, which wins over both. Ask `graphics.can_draw()`.
- **A new `ImageData` every frame.** Every decode or `from_rgb` is a new picture, and a new picture is worked out again. Clone the one in your state; a clone is the same picture and costs a reference count.
